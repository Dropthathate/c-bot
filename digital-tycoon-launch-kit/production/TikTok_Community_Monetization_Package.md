# TikTok Community Monetization Package

## Operator’s Room: Subscription and Series Plan

> **Account-review note.** The available browser session reached TikTok’s sign-in screen, so the account’s current eligibility and exact control labels could not be verified. This package is structured against TikTok’s public Creator Academy documentation current as of August 2026. Before launch, compare the available in-app perks, pricing tiers, and moderation prompts inside **Profile → TikTok Studio → Monetization Programs → Subscription**. TikTok currently describes Subscription as a broader community product with exclusive posts, subscriber badges, personalized stickers, and creator-defined perks; it distinguishes the LIVE-only Super Fan program separately. [1] [2]

## 1. Subscription Positioning

**Name:** **Operator’s Room**

**One-line promise:** A bold, private strategy room for independent creators who want sharper LIVE presence, stronger boundaries, and better monetization moves without performing for free.

The public account should remain the stage: sharp micro-lessons, confident hooks, visible energy, and short examples. The Operator’s Room is the deeper layer: priority attention, a direct point of view, behind-the-scenes decision-making, and recurring chances to shape what happens in the next LIVE. It should feel like being close to the person who runs the room, not like buying a vague “VIP” label.

| Decision | Recommended launch setting | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Subscription price | **$5.99/month** | This is TikTok’s documented default tier and stays accessible while the creator proves a consistent delivery rhythm. [1] |
| Review point | Day 30 | Retain the price if the schedule is sustainable and the first ten subscribers receive every promised perk. |
| Public promise | “Get closer to the strategy, the show, and the next move.” | Clear benefit without promising income, legal outcomes, personal access on demand, or unlimited advice. |
| Delivery standard | One sub-only post, two priority-comment windows, and three scheduled LIVE moments each week | The offer is valuable because it is recurring and predictable—not because it is overpromised. |

## 2. Subscriber Badge System

TikTok’s current materials describe a Subscriber badge next to a community member’s name; the account should configure the badge option the in-app tool presents. The following names are the **community identity system** to use in overlays, pinned comment language, shout-outs, and sub-only art. They are not a claim that TikTok will permit fully custom badge graphics. [2]

| Community level | Visual concept | What the host says on LIVE | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Room Key** | A tiny brass key with a black diamond head | “Room Keys, I see you. You’re in the building.” | New subscriber; access and recognition, not hierarchy based on spending. |
| **Gold Ledger** | An open black notebook with a brass rule | “Gold Ledger energy: take the note, then make the move.” | Consistent participant who shows up and uses the prompts. |
| **Live Operator** | Headset silhouette inside the four-bracket mark | “Operators, drop your check-in—what are we building tonight?” | Community regular who participates constructively in LIVE discussion. |
| **Board Builder** | Four brass bracket corners forming a board | “Board Builders help this room run cleaner.” | Helpful contributor, selected on usefulness and conduct, never spending volume. |

> **Operating rule:** Award recognition for contribution, clear communication, respectful engagement, and completed challenges. Never frame a person as “better” because they spent more, gifted more, or paid for more access.

The art direction should match the sales-page system: black, bone, Operator Brass, clean outlines, no sexualized body imagery, and no gambling imagery. The set must be legible at small chat sizes, so every emote should use one central icon and no text smaller than a single word.

## 4. Weekly LIVE Operating Schedule

Use the account’s local time zone and lock the schedule for four consecutive weeks. TikTok’s public materials recommend tailoring perks to what can actually be delivered; consistency will convert better than adding more slots. [2]

| Session | Suggested time | Public layer | Subscriber layer | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday — Money Move Check-In** | 8:30 p.m. | A 30-minute public reset: what is working, what is being cut, one actionable move. | Subscribers receive the priority question queue and the pinned “Room Notes” summary. | Opens the week with a clear execution focus. |
| **Wednesday — Build the Room** | 9:00 p.m. | Live breakdown of a hook, offer, menu, or community situation. | Subscribers vote on the next breakdown and get a short sub-only recap video. | Creates participation and a repeat reason to return. |
| **Saturday — After Hours Operator Session** | 10:00 p.m. | High-energy public conversation with a defined theme and boundary. | First 15 minutes are subscriber-first: check-in, question priority, and next-week topic vote. | Rewards subscribers without making the public LIVE feel empty. |

The host opens every session with one sentence that makes the room’s rules visible: *“Public room gets the lesson; Operator’s Room gets the priority queue, the recap, and the next-move vote. Everything is optional—pick the lane that fits you.”*

## 5. First-30-Day Subscriber Rollout

| Week | Primary job | Public content | Subscriber delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Introduce the room | One public post explaining the promise, one LIVE invitation, and a single pinned comment about access. | Welcome post, Room Key check-in, and one priority-comment window. |
| **Week 2** | Prove the rhythm | Show a short public before/after of a LIVE menu or hook. | Sub-only breakdown of the full decision behind it, plus a Wednesday topic vote. |
| **Week 3** | Create ownership | Run a public micro-challenge with a clear action. | Let subscribers choose the Saturday challenge prompt and recognize useful participation. |
| **Week 4** | Retain and improve | Share a public recap of a lesson learned from the month. | Ask one structured question: “What should the Operator’s Room keep, cut, or build next?” |

Avoid promising direct messages, individual consulting, legal advice, guaranteed income, unlimited reviews, or on-demand access. The room is premium because it has structure, consistency, and a distinct point of view—not because the creator is available at all hours.

# Premium TikTok Series

## **Family-Court Readiness: Document, Communicate, Prepare**

> **Legal note:** This Series should be positioned as general educational organization and self-management content, not legal advice. It must tell viewers that court rules, evidence rules, deadlines, and safety requirements differ by location; they should get advice from a qualified local lawyer or legal-aid organization for their own matter. The content must not encourage retaliation, harassment, evasion of court orders, coached testimony, fabrication, or secret recording where prohibited.

**Recommended Series price:** **$39 one-time**.

The price is deliberately below the user’s $1–$190 range ceiling because the Series is a focused, short-form educational organizer—not individualized representation, a legal document service, or an outcome guarantee. At launch, use $39 rather than discount pressure. Reassess only after the creator can show the Series has stable production quality, clear disclaimers, and a support workload they can maintain.

TikTok Series supports collections of premium videos behind a paywall, with up to 80 videos and a free introduction video. TikTok says each video can run from 30 seconds to 20 minutes, and purchases can be made from an in-video link or the profile. Build this as 12 vertical videos, roughly 5–9 minutes each, with an honest free trailer that explains scope and limits. [3] [4]

| # | Episode title | Core learning objective | Practical asset or prompt |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Start Here: What This Series Is—and Is Not** | Set the educational scope, safety boundaries, and local-law disclaimer before any action. | “Questions for my local attorney / legal-aid clinic” page. |
| 2 | **The First 72 Hours: Stabilize Before You Respond** | Separate immediate safety, childcare, housing, and legal-support needs from reactive posting or messaging. | 72-hour stability checklist. |
| 3 | **Build the Timeline, Not a Spiral** | Turn scattered memory into a dated, neutral chronology with sources and unknowns marked clearly. | Chronology template: date, event, source, impact, follow-up. |
| 4 | **Document What You Can Lawfully Document** | Explain the difference between preserving existing records and creating risky or unlawful evidence. | Evidence inventory with “original / copy / source / relevance” columns. |
| 5 | **Communication That Does Not Feed the Fire** | Teach concise, factual, child- or issue-focused communication and when to stop engaging. | Before-send checklist: brief, factual, neutral, necessary. |
| 6 | **Keep Orders and Deadlines in One Place** | Make a working calendar for hearings, exchanges, filing deadlines, and document requests without interpreting them. | Court-event calendar page. |
| 7 | **Your Support Team: Lawyer, Legal Aid, Advocate, Therapist** | Identify what each role can and cannot do; prepare focused questions for each. | Support-map worksheet. |
| 8 | **Prepare for the Meeting, Not the Movie Scene** | Replace courtroom-performance expectations with a preparation list: documents, questions, logistics, and attire. | Meeting-prep one-pager. |
| 9 | **The Parenting and Caregiving Record** | Organize factual caregiving, school, medical, and routine information without exaggeration. | Caregiving log with source references. |
| 10 | **Money, Records, and Practical Capacity** | Collect financial and household records responsibly; identify gaps for a qualified adviser. | Document-request inventory. |
| 11 | **Protect Your Digital Footprint** | Pause reactive posting, review privacy, and preserve relevant public material without turning social media into a battleground. | Digital-boundary checklist. |
| 12 | **The Case-Readiness Binder** | Assemble a calm, navigable case file and set a 30-minute weekly maintenance routine. | Binder index and weekly reset ritual. |

## Series Trailer Script

> “If family court or an accusation has thrown your life into chaos, your first move is not to post harder, argue longer, or build a story in panic. Your first move is to get organized. This Series walks you through a calm, practical readiness system: timelines, records, communication boundaries, support-team questions, and a case binder you can actually use. It is not legal advice, and it will not tell you what result you will get. It will help you arrive more prepared for the professional help and court process in front of you.”

## Series Page Description

**A practical readiness system for people navigating family-court stress and allegations.** Build a neutral timeline, preserve records responsibly, communicate with more control, organize your questions for qualified help, and create a case-readiness binder. This is general education—not legal advice, legal representation, or a promise of a court result.

## Production Rules

Film vertically at 1080p or higher, use clean audio, keep the background quiet, and place the disclaimer in the opening trailer, the Series description, and a short closing card on episodes touching evidence, orders, or communication. TikTok’s Series guidance calls for professional-quality vertical video and commercial-use music where music is used. [3]
