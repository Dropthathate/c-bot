# 14-Day TikTok Traffic Calendar

## The Digital Tycoon Traffic Engine

> **Execution rule:** Every script opens with its **hook + twist on screen and spoken in the first 0.5 seconds**. Film vertically, frame the face or primary visual tightly, add burned-in captions, and use one visual shift around the 4–6 second mark. The public post gives a complete micro-lesson; the call to action directs the viewer to one next move only.

> **Legal-content rule for Days 8–14:** These posts are educational organization and self-management content, not legal advice. Never claim to predict court results, tell viewers to violate an order, coach testimony, hide information, retaliate, harass someone, or record people where it is unlawful. Add a short caption line such as: “General education only—get local legal advice for your own matter.”

## Days 1–7: Payhip Playbook Traffic

### Day 1 — Stop Going LIVE for Free

**Format:** Tight face-to-camera, black-and-brass desk, hold a blank menu card.

**0.0–0.5 second hook-twist:** “You are not undercharging on LIVE—you are leaving the room unbuilt.”

**15–30 second script:** “If people watch, comment, and disappear, the problem is not your audience. You gave them a show with no role. Start every LIVE with three lanes: a free move, an access move, and a reason to come back. The free move could be a vote. The access move could be the worksheet or focused question queue. The return move is next week’s room. That is how you stop hoping people support you and start giving them a reason to participate.”

**On-screen close:** “A room needs roles.”

Track three things instead: did they move, did they use it, and did they return? Your post has one job. Your LIVE has one job. Your offer has one job. ### Day 8 — Build a Timeline, Not a Spiral

**Format:** Neutral desk shot of a dated chronology sheet; no case documents or personal names visible.

**0.0–0.5 second hook-twist:** “If your story is a spiral, your first move is not to explain harder—it is to build a timeline.”

**15–30 second script:** “When stress is high, everything feels connected at once. Start with date, event, source, and follow-up. Keep facts separate from what you remember, and mark anything you need to verify. A timeline does not decide your case. It gives you a calmer way to prepare questions for qualified help and to stop losing important details in emotional messages.”

**On-screen close:** “Date / event / source / follow-up.”

### Day 9 — Preserve, Do Not Perform

**Format:** Hold a phone face-down beside a folder labeled `ORIGINALS`; use a lock icon overlay.

**0.0–0.5 second hook-twist:** “You do not need a perfect speech right now—you need to preserve what already exists.”

**15–30 second script:** “Before you start posting, forwarding, or arguing, pause. Save original records responsibly, note where they came from, and keep a simple inventory of what you have. Do not edit screenshots until the original is preserved. Do not create drama to create evidence. And do not record anyone unless you know it is lawful where you are. Calm preservation beats a public performance every time.”

**On-screen close:** “Original / source / relevance.”

### Day 10 — Do Not Reply From the Adrenaline

**Format:** Show a large text bubble that gets replaced by a three-line neutral reply template.

If not, step away and write the version you could stand behind tomorrow. This is not about being weak or silent. It is about choosing a record that is clear instead of feeding a fight you cannot control.”

### Day 12 — Your Support Team Has Different Jobs

A therapist supports your wellbeing; they are not your legal strategist. Before your next appointment, write the exact question each person is qualified to answer.
